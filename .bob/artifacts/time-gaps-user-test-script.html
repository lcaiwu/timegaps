Time Gaps — User Test Script

Feature: Time Gaps (IntelliMagic) Prototype: Live React prototype — open in browser before session Version: Revised 8/13 · Grounded in Getpages prototype

What the Prototype Shows

The prototype is a single report called "Getpages Change Over Time" for Db2 Data Sharing Group DB2H. It lives inside IBM Z IntelliMagic Vision for z/OS under Db2 → Statistics → DB Statistics.

What is Getpages? Getpages is a Db2 metric that counts how many database pages were read during a time interval. It's a core indicator of database workload — engineers watch it to understand how hard Db2 is working and to spot unusual dips or spikes.

The chart plots actual Getpages per day against a baseline (expected value) and ±2 standard deviation bands. When the red actual line falls outside the yellow SD bands, that signals something unusual in Db2 activity — either unexpectedly high or low page reads.

The problem under test: the chart has blank/flat areas that can mean three completely different things, and they all look the same:

Filtered — the user's selection excluded these days (e.g. specific time windows were filtered out). Data exists but was intentionally removed from view.
System outage / missing data — the Db2 telemetry collector was down. No data was ever recorded. The line simply breaks.
Zero value — the collector was running but Getpages was literally zero. The system may have been idle or a specific subsystem stopped processing reads. Shown as a hollow circle on the x-axis.

Background & Objectives

Problem: Because these three states look the same on the chart, users can't tell whether a blank period means "I filtered that out," "the system was down," or "Db2 really processed zero pages." This creates confusion and erodes trust in the data.

Test Objectives: Understand how users interpret the Time Gaps design on the Getpages chart and the Selection summary. Identify what to prioritize, what to improve, and what unmet needs exist.

Prototype Setup — Facilitator Checklist

Complete before the participant joins.
  • Open the prototype in Chrome or Firefox at full-screen width (≥ 1280 px).
  • Log in: click any button on the login screen to enter the dashboard.
  • The app opens to the Getpages Change Over Time report for DB2H.
  • The Selection card (blue box, top-right of report header) should read: 08/26/2026 · IGT, All sysplexes · Hourly · Thu
  • The Chart tab is active (not Grid) and the legend is visible on the right.
  • The Edit panel drawer is closed.
  • Task 4 only: before that task, open Edit panel → Data gaps → Zeros = Show, Outage = Show shading → drag Time window sliders to full 13-month range.

Tasks

Task 1 Overall UI Comprehension First impressions · mental model
Goal

Understand the participant's first impressions of the Getpages report screen and their mental model of what it's for — before directing them to any specific element.

What's on Screen
  • Top nav: "IBM Z IntelliMagic Vision for z/OS" — Dashboard / Edit Dashboard / Create Report / Selection / Filter / Export
  • Left sidebar: Navigation tree expanded to CF and XCF → XCF → Usage by System (highlighted in blue)
  • Thumbnail strip: 10 small chart previews of the report set — MAXMSG highlighted in red
  • Breadcrumb: Db2 → Statistics → DB Statistics → Getpages Change Over Time
  • Report header: Title "Getpages Change Over Time / for DB2 Data Sharing Group DB2H", a Description block with a red warning icon, and the blue Selection card
  • Chart: Red actual Getpages line, amber expected line, ±2 SD shaded bands, hatched orange regions (filtered), line breaks (outages), hollow red circles (zeros)
  • Legend (right): Actual value, Expected value, +2 STD, -2 STD, ±2 STD, Null, System failure
Setting the Scene
"I'm going to show you a screen. Take as much time as you need — just tell me what you see, what you think it's for, and what you would do first if this were your own environment."
Observe (do not prompt)
  • Where do they look first — the chart, the sidebar, the header?
  • Do they notice the blank/gap areas without being directed?
  • What words do they use — "missing data," "filtered," "errors," something else?
  • What do they ignore entirely?
Follow-up Questions
  • "Walk me through what you think this screen is for."
  • "What's the most important piece of information on this screen to you?"
  • "If you were using this in your day-to-day, what would you want visible by default — and what could be hidden or optional?"
  • "Are there any filter details you'd always want to see? What about ones you'd tuck away?"
  • "What feels like it belongs here, and what feels like it might be extra?"
Task 2 Identify Time Gaps in the Chart Gap recognition · interpretation
Goal

Evaluate whether users can spot the different gap types in the Getpages chart, interpret what they mean, and how quickly and naturally this happens.

What's in the Chart
Hatched orange bands — filtered periods. These days were excluded by the user's selection (e.g. specific time windows or days of week). Getpages data exists but isn't shown.
Line breaks, no shading — system outages. The Db2 telemetry collector was down; no Getpages data was collected. The line simply stops and restarts.
Hollow red circles on the x-axis — zero values. Getpages was literally 0 that day. The collector ran, but no page reads were recorded.

Hovering shows a tooltip: Expected value / Current (shows "— (no data)" for outages, "0 Getpages" for zeros) / ±2 std dev range.

Setting the Scene
"You're reviewing the Getpages report for your Db2 system, checking activity over the last quarter. Take a look at the chart and tell me what you notice."
Before They Interact — Ask First
"Before you click or hover anything — what would you expect to see if you selected one of those flat or blank areas?"
Observe (do not prompt)
  • Do they spot the gaps without being directed?
  • How long does it take?
  • What do they say out loud — do they call it "missing data," "a gap," "filtered out," something else?
  • Do they hover, click, or try to scroll?
  • Do they look at the legend to make sense of it?
  • Do they notice the difference between the orange hatched areas vs. the plain line breaks?
⚑ If they hover a gap: watch whether the tooltip text ("— (no data)" vs "0 Getpages") registers as meaningful or just noise. Note their reaction.
Follow-up Questions
  • "What does that blank/gap area mean to you?"
  • "Does it feel different from the rest of the chart — or does it blend in?"
  • "Is there anything about it that surprises you, or that you expected to see that isn't there?"
  • "If you spotted this in your actual Db2 monitoring work, what would you do next?"
  • "If there were multiple different reasons a gap could appear — like a filter versus an outage versus the system going idle — how would you want to tell them apart visually?"
Task 3 Selection Summary Comprehension Readability · clarity of what was filtered
Goal

Understand how users read and interpret the report's summary information — specifically the blue Selection card and the Description block — and whether it tells them what they need to know about what's included or excluded.

What's on Screen
  • Blue Selection card (top-right of report header): shows the active filters — date (08/26/2026), Interest Groups (IGT, All sysplexes), Interval (Hourly), Days (Thu). Clicking it opens the full Selection Panel.
  • Description block (left of the blue card): red warning icon + text: "Some telemetry data is missing due to a network maintenance outage. This report shows the Getpages metric… Values outside these ranges suggest unusual activity."
  • If they click the blue card: the Selection Panel modal opens with four tabs — Time Range, Additional Filters, Comparisons, Saved Combinations. Let them explore freely.
Setting the Scene
"Now look at the summary area at the top of the report — the blue card and the text next to it. Based on what's shown, tell me what you understand about what's included in this chart and what might have been left out."
Before They Read — Ask First
"Before you read it — what would you expect a summary like this to tell you about a Getpages report?"
Observe (do not prompt)
  • Do they read the Description text or skip straight to the blue card?
  • Do they notice the red warning icon? Do they react to it?
  • Do they scan top to bottom, or jump to specific pieces?
  • Do they click the blue card to get more detail?
  • If they open the Selection Panel: which tab do they go to first? What are they looking for?
  • Any re-reads, pauses, or visible confusion?
Follow-up Questions
  • "What does this tell you about what's in this chart?"
  • "Does it tell you why certain parts of the chart are blank or missing?"
  • "Is there anything here that feels unnecessary — something you'd remove?"
  • "Is there anything you expected to see here that isn't?"
  • "How would you describe this summary to a colleague who hasn't seen this screen before?"
  • "Is there anything harder to understand than it should be?"
Task 4 Extreme Use Case Design stress test · scale
Goal

Stress-test the design when the Getpages chart is almost entirely covered in gaps. Validate whether gap types stay distinguishable at scale and understand users' mental models when nearly all data is affected.

Set Up Before This Task
  1. Click Edit report (blue button, top-right) to open the Edit panel drawer on the right.
  2. Under Data gaps: set Zeros = Show, Outage = Show shading.
  3. Under Time window: drag both sliders to show the full 13-month range (Aug '25 – Aug '26). The chart now shows dense clusters of orange hatched bands and line breaks spread across the whole time series — roughly 70% of days are affected.
  4. Leave the drawer open so participants can see the gap summary counter at the bottom ("X of Y weeks have gaps").
Setting the Scene
"Now imagine you're reviewing Getpages for a Db2 system that had a lot of maintenance activity — heavy scheduled exclusions across the year, plus several collector outages. For example: Getpages data excluded Monday through Friday from 9am to 2pm and 4pm to 8pm, every third Tuesday, and Wednesdays from 7pm to midnight. Take a look at the chart."
Before They Look — Ask First
"Before you look — if a Db2 report had that many exclusions and outages, what would you expect the Getpages chart to look like?"
Observe (do not prompt)
  • What's their first reaction to the density of gaps across the chart?
  • Do they try to read the chart, or do they immediately feel it's unreadable?
  • Do they distinguish between the orange hatched areas (filtered) and the plain breaks (outages)?
  • Do they notice or use the Edit panel controls — the gap summary, the window slider, the zeros/outage toggles?
  • What language do they use — "excluded," "missing," "down," "filtered"?
Follow-up Questions
  • "Walk me through what you're seeing."
  • "How would you normally approach a Getpages chart that looked like this?"
  • "Is there anything here that makes it harder to understand what happened during those time periods?"
  • "What would make this easier to work with when there are this many gaps?"
  • "If you had to explain to someone on your team why some gaps are orange-hatched and others are plain breaks — what would you say, based on what you see here?"

Closing Questions

Ask at the end of every session regardless of how the tasks went.

  • "Is there anything you noticed during the session that you didn't get a chance to mention?"
  • "If you could change one thing about what you saw today, what would it be?"
  • "What, if anything, would make you trust the Getpages data in this report more?"

Post-Session Synthesis Guide

Use after all sessions are complete.

1. Pain points and frustrations

What moments caused confusion, hesitation, or negative reactions — especially around the blank/gap areas on the Getpages chart?

2. Unmet needs

What did participants expect to see or do — for example, information they looked for in the Selection summary that wasn't there?

3. Patterns across participants

Where did multiple participants use the same language, make the same mistake, or behave the same way when encountering a gap?

4. Design direction

What should be prioritized, iterated, or reconsidered? What questions remain open for a follow-up round of testing?
